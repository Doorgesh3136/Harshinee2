The House

Six yards of a story

Harshinee began with a simple idea: the sarees our mothers kept folded in muslin should still be worn, and loved, in Mauritius today.

We travel to the weaving towns ourselves and choose each piece by hand, from heavy bridal silks to the soft cottons you reach for every week. Then we bring them home to our two boutiques, where you can touch the zari, try the drape and take your time.

Three looms we love

Every region weaves its own saree. These are the three we return to most.

Varanasi · Banarasi

Fine silk woven with gold and silver zari in floral buttis and Mughal patterns. The saree of North Indian weddings.

Shop Banarasi

Kanchipuram · Kanjeevaram

Heavy mulberry silk with contrast temple borders, the body and border often woven separately and joined by hand.

Shop Kanjeevaram

Bengal · Tant & handloom

Crisp, airy cottons woven on handlooms, like the white saree with a red border worn at Durga Puja.

How we choose

From the loom to Lallmatie

  1. We go to the weaversWe buy in the weaving towns, so we see the silk, the zari and the finish before anything travels.
  2. We check every pieceEach saree is opened, checked along its length and folded in muslin before it reaches the boutique.
  3. We help you style itBlouse, jewellery and drape: we put the full look together with you in store or on WhatsApp.

Four ways to drape

The same six yards, worn four different ways across India.

Nivi

The most worn drape: pleats tucked at the front, pallu over the left shoulder.

Bengali

Box pleats and a pallu brought over both shoulders, often with keys tied at the end.

Gujarati

Seedha pallu: the pallu comes over the right shoulder and spreads across the front.

Nauvari

The Maharashtrian nine-yard drape, tucked between the legs like a dhoti.

Saree care

Keep it for the next generation

A good silk lasts a lifetime. A few habits keep the colour and the zari bright.

  • Dry clean silkSilks, velvets and zari work should always be dry cleaned.
  • Wrap in muslinCotton or muslin lets silk breathe. Avoid plastic covers.
  • Refold oftenChange the folds every few months so the zari does not crack.
  • No perfume on zariSpray perfume before you drape, never on the gold threads.
  • Air it outAfter wearing, hang it in the shade for a few hours before folding.
  • Iron gentlyLow heat, on the reverse, with a cloth between the iron and the silk.
